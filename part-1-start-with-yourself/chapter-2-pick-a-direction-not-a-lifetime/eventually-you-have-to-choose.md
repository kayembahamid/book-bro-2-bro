# Eventually You Have To Choose

At some point, research stops being research.

It becomes hiding.

I saw that happen with a guy at university.

He kept saying he was still deciding what course to study. But after a while, it didn't look like deciding anymore.

It looked like fear wearing the clothes of preparation.

He spent almost two years trying to decide. Not two months. Two years.

Every time he thought he had decided, he found another option. Another video. Another reason to doubt himself. Comparing. Changing his mind. Starting again.

I remember thinking: "How much longer is this going to continue?"

Because he wasn't getting closer to a decision. He was just getting better at avoiding one.

There's no perfect choice. No perfect degree. No perfect career. No perfect opportunity.

A decision is often just the best choice you can make with the information you have today.

And sometimes that has to be enough.

We tend to think clarity comes first. Then action.

But a lot of the time, it works the other way around. You choose. Then you learn. Then you adjust.

Some lessons only appear after you start moving.

That's why I tell students not to obsess over making the perfect decision. Pick a direction. Move. Learn. Adjust.

Because a person moving in the wrong direction can still make corrections. A person standing still can't.

Before making an important decision, pause and ask yourself:

Do I understand the real problem?

Have I gathered enough information?

Have I explored enough options?

Do I understand what this choice might cost?

If the answer is yes, then there comes a point where you have to stop researching and start moving.

Perfect decisions don't exist. But progress does.

And progress usually begins when you stop waiting for certainty and take the next step.
