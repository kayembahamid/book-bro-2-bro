# Chapter 1 : You Don’t Need To Know Everything Yet

"Ham, can I ask you something?"

I'm at my desk trying to finish my lesson notes so I can finally get to lunch. Everyone else has gone. He's still standing there.

"Sure."

He pulls up a chair. Then he asks the question I've heard a hundred times.

"Ham, what am I supposed to do after school?"

At first I think he means graduation. University. A job. Something like that.

But the longer we talk, the more I realize he isn't asking about school at all.

He's asking about life.

What should I do? What if I choose wrong? What if everyone else works it out before me? What if I mess the whole thing up?

Some students ask it straight. Others bury it inside a different question. Underneath, it's the same fear every time.

And I don't think school prepares anyone for it.

School teaches you to pass tests. Write essays. Solve equations. No one sits you down and explains how to build a life.

You get: "Work hard." "Get good grades." "Be successful."

Successful according to who? Your parents? Your friends? The person online making their life look perfect?

You can spend years chasing somebody else's version of success and only notice at the end that you're living a life you never chose.

So direction matters. Not because you need every step mapped out, because you won't have that. Not because you need a perfect plan, because there isn't one. It matters because life gets easier when you know roughly what kind of person you're trying to become.

I'm not going to tell you what to do. I can't. No one can.

But I can help you think. About direction. About choices. About the pressure to have it all worked out too early.

You don't need to know everything yet. You just need to start paying attention to where you're pointed.

And trust me. Almost no one has it figured out.
