# Most Things Already Exist

#### Better Is Often Enough

You don't always need to be first.

That took me a while to understand. The first book isn't always the best book. The first course isn't always the clearest course. The first app isn't always the easiest app to use.

The person who wins is usually the one who made it better.

I've stopped myself from starting things because someone else had already done them. The website already existed. The course already existed. The book already existed. The business already existed. The app already existed.

So I assumed there was no point.

But "already done" doesn't always mean "done well."

The more I looked around, the more obvious it became. A lot of successful work wasn't completely new. It was simply better in some way. A little clearer. A little faster. A little easier to use. A little more helpful. A little more honest.

That changed how I looked at things. For a long time, I thought success came from having a brilliant idea. Now I think a lot of it comes from execution.

Actually building something. Testing it. Improving it. Listening to feedback. Making adjustments. Then doing it again.

The internet can make every idea feel already taken. Search for anything, and someone has probably done it. A course. A channel. A product. A business. A book.

At first, that can feel discouraging. Then you realize something. People are looking for useful. Something that solves a problem. Something that works. Something that feels better than what they had before.

Eventually, I stopped worrying so much about: "Has somebody done this before?" And started paying more attention to: "Can this be improved? Can this be made more useful?"

Because many chances don't come from inventing something completely new. They come from improving something that already exists.

And sometimes, better is enough.
