# What Can I Create?

For most of your life, you're trained to consume. Read the book. Watch the video. Use the app. Buy the product. Scroll through the feed.

Then one day, you try to create something yourself. And the world starts looking different.

A book no longer looks like just a book. You start thinking about the person who wrote it. The structure. The decisions. The edits. The mistakes. The hours behind it.

A website no longer looks like just a website. You start wondering how it was built. Why it was designed that way. What problem it's trying to solve. What could be improved.

That's what creating does to you. It changes what you notice.

The week after I started writing my first book, I couldn't read other books the same way. I kept seeing the choices behind them. Why this chapter came first. Why that line was cut. The work I'd never noticed as a reader.

Ideas start appearing in ordinary places. A problem someone keeps complaining about. A tool that could work better. A process that takes too long. A question that keeps coming up.

Things you'd have ignored before start standing out.

Behind every finished thing, someone made decisions. Someone made mistakes. Someone adjusted. Someone tried to solve a problem.

The world starts looking less like a collection of products. And more like a collection of solutions.

Every book exists because someone decided to write it. Every business exists because someone decided to start it. Every tool exists because someone saw a problem and tried to solve it.

Creating something changes how you think. You don't have to become an entrepreneur for that to be true.

Instead of only asking: "What can I get?" You start asking: "What can I contribute?"

And that question has a way of opening doors you never noticed before. Because people who create value look at the world differently.

They're looking for something to build.
