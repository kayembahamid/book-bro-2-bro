# People Remember How You Show Up

Your name means something before you walk into the room. To a teacher. A manager. A colleague.

Someone hears it and quietly remembers what you were like to work with. Reliable. Careless. Helpful. Difficult. Easy to trust.

That story gets written long before you need anyone to speak for you.

A student once asked me to be a reference. I said yes straight away. I didn't have to think about it, because I already knew what I'd say.

She showed up. She worked hard. When she said she'd do something, she did it. When she got something wrong, she owned it.

That was enough.

We spend a lot of time worrying about CVs. Certificates. Qualifications. Those matter. But your name carries more than what's printed on paper.

Teachers remember. So do managers, friends and colleagues. They remember who followed through. Who kept their word. Who could be trusted.

Sometimes a chance appears because of a skill. Sometimes because somebody remembered your name.

Which is why reputation gets built long before you need it.

People are always forming an opinion. Not loudly. Not officially. But they notice. How you work. How you speak. How you handle mistakes. How you treat people when nobody important is watching.

None of it is complicated.

Show up. Do your best. Keep your word. Treat people well.

Someone will remember.
