# Opportunities Rarely Arrive The Way You Expect

"I can't find any jobs."

Sometimes that sentence is true. But sometimes the problem isn't that there are no jobs. Sometimes the problem is the search is too narrow.

One job title. One search term. One version of what the opportunity is supposed to look like.

And if that exact door doesn't open, it starts to feel like there are no doors left.

I've heard students say this many times. So I usually ask: "What are you searching for?"

Sometimes the answer is the problem.

One student kept searching for "cybersecurity analyst." Nothing near him. But "IT support," "help desk," "junior security," "SOC trainee" were all sitting right there under different names. Same skills he was building. Different labels.

The chance isn't always missing. Sometimes the search is just too narrow.

It's easy to do this. Not just with jobs. With life.

You become attached to one version of success. One company. One career path. One opening. One door.

And when that door doesn't open, it feels like everything is closed.

But chances rarely arrive exactly the way you imagined.

The internship can become the full-time job. The side project can become the business. A conversation can become a way in. An introduction can change everything.

Many chances come through someone who remembers you. A friend hears about an opening. A former teacher remembers your name. Someone recommends you. Someone thinks of you when something appears.

We're remembered for more than we think. Who showed up. Who worked hard. Who treated others well. Who was easy to work with.

That's why I've never really liked the word networking. It sounds too strategic. Too fake. Too much like using people.

Most of the time, it's simpler than that. Have real conversations. Build relationships. Stay in touch. Help when you can. Let others know what you're trying to do.

Because the chance you need may not arrive wearing the name you expected.

And that's exactly why you might miss it.
