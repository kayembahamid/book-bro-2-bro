# Chapter 4 : Put Yourself Where Opportunity Can See You

### No One Knows Who You Are Yet.

You can be talented and still invisible.

That's one of the first hard lessons after school.

You may have skills. You may be serious. You may be ready to work. But if no one knows what you can do, the world doesn't come looking for you.

I learned that when I started applying for my first jobs.

I sent out applications. Then waited. Nothing. No replies. No interviews. No calls.

At first, I was frustrated. I kept checking my email. Refreshing my inbox. Wondering why no one was replying.

Then it hit me. No one knew who I was. There was no office somewhere with a person looking for Ham, digging through CVs for my name.

I was the one looking for opportunities. Which meant I had to go and find them.

A lot of us leave school thinking life works the same way school does. A teacher tells you what chapter comes next. An exam tells you what to study. A timetable tells you where to be.

Then school ends. And suddenly the structure disappears. No reminders. No instructions. No next chapter.

You have to start looking. You have to introduce yourself. You have to send the application. You have to follow up. You have to keep going when no one replies.

That's the part a lot of us aren't prepared for.

The world may be full of opportunities. But opportunity doesn't automatically know your name.

Learning a skill is one challenge. Getting someone to notice that skill is another.

You can be hardworking and still unknown. You can be ready and still unseen.

That doesn't mean you're not good enough. It means people don't know what you can do yet.

So you can't just sit back and wait for opportunity to find you. You have to put yourself where opportunity can see you.
