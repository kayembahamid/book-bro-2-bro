# What Is Actually Bothering You?

Sometimes your feelings tell the truth, but not the whole truth.

They tell you something is wrong. They make you pay attention. They make you want to change something.

But they don't always point to the real problem first.

I learned that during one of my first sales jobs.

At the time, I thought: "I hate this job."

That felt like the problem. The job.

But when I slowed down, I started noticing something else. I didn't hate everything. I didn't hate talking to people. I didn't hate learning sales. I didn't even hate working hard.

I hated the pressure. The rejection. The feeling of not knowing what I was doing.

That was different. The job wasn't the whole problem. The real problem was hiding underneath the feeling.

I've seen this happen a lot.

Someone says: "I hate studying." But maybe they hate one subject.

Someone says: "My boss hates me." But maybe the communication is bad.

Someone says: "I'm terrible with money." But maybe they never made a plan.

Feelings are useful. They tell us something is wrong. But they don't always tell us exactly what's wrong.

Before you make a big decision, slow down. Ask yourself: "What is actually bothering me?"

Remember the student who wanted to quit university? Same lesson, smaller scale. Name the real thing before you act on it.

Because if you solve the wrong problem, you can change everything and still feel stuck.

The problem might not be the problem. Sometimes it's only pointing to the real one.
