# Failure Is Information

Failure is painful because it feels personal.

A rejected application can feel like rejection of you. A failed exam can feel like proof that you're not smart. A mistake can feel like evidence that you're not ready.

But failure doesn't always tell you who you are. Sometimes it's only telling you what needs attention.

You fail an exam. Get rejected for a job. Apply for something and hear nothing back. Start something and it doesn't work.

And the first thought is usually: "Maybe I'm not good enough."

I've had that thought too. But over time I started seeing failure differently.

Failure doesn't actually tell you who you are. It tells you what happened.

The failed interview shows you something. The rejected application gives feedback. The business idea that didn't work shows you something. The mistake points to something you missed.

The question is whether you're willing to look at it.

Because every setback carries information. What worked? What didn't? What should I do differently next time?

Failure isn't a verdict. It's feedback.

The only question is whether you'll read it.
