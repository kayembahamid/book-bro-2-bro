# Your Reputation Follows You

Every job leaves something behind.

Not just money. Not just experience. Not just another line on your CV. It leaves an impression. What your name means to someone. Whether you made things easier. Whether you could be trusted. Whether someone would want to work with you again.

Potential means very little if nobody has seen it.

I've worked with smart people. Talented people. Capable people. People with great ideas. But when chances appeared, they didn't always get them.

At first, I thought promotions were mostly about talent. Then work showed me something else.

People promote who they trust. Can they rely on you? Can they give you responsibility? Can they count on you when things get difficult?

That's what the people around you are really paying attention to. Not just what you can do. But whether they trust you to do it.

Your reputation follows you.

The manager you work for today may become a reference tomorrow. The coworker sitting next to you today may work at a different company next year. The person you help today may open a door years later.

When you're young, jobs can feel separate. One job. Then another. Then another.

But life doesn't really work that way. Names come back. Conversations matter. The impression you leave travels further than you think.

Every job gives you two things. Skills. And a reputation. The skills help you do the next job. The reputation helps you get it.

Years later, someone may forget exactly what work you did. But they'll remember whether they trusted you.

And trust has a way of creating chances long after the job is over.
